Herald: a day with your mail

Mail and calendar in one villain app, for the Lazarski and Code Villain accounts on Uberspace and the calendar on Radicale. Work mail stays in Outlook. Monday, Nov 3. Night and day, with the Nightshade tokens. Herald is a working name, and the content is made up.

1Your day, then your mail

Monday, 08:40, with coffee. Herald opens on one Inbox for both accounts, with today's calendar above it.

  • My choice: one Inbox for both accounts. Each account wears one colour on its senders, and its calendar wears the same one.
  • My choice: today sits above the Inbox, 8 to 22, with a line for now. The week is in Calendar.
  • My choice: a mail opens in the list's place, and Esc goes back. J and K move, E archives, R replies.
  • My choice: one field at the foot of every view, to ask Herald something or tell it what to do. Claude Code on this Mac answers, as in Almanac.

2You tell it how to sort

You type “Put GitHub and Forgejo mail in a folder called Code”. Herald makes the rule, moves what's already there, and says what it did.

  • My choice: the rule lives on the mail server (Sieve, which Uberspace runs), so it sorts while the Mac is shut, and your iPhone sees the same folders.
  • My choice: it says what it did in words, with Undo. There's no rule editor: to change a rule, you say so (“Dependabot too”).
  • My choice: sorting that needs reading, like “anything that looks like an invoice”, can't be a server rule. Herald does that while it's open on this Mac, and says so when it makes one.
  • My choice: a folder with the same name in both accounts shows once.

3You write, then make it more professional

Anna Weber asks whether Layer works for a team. You answer the way you'd text, then press More professional.

  • My choice: the button sits beside Send. It rewrites in place, in the language you wrote in, and keeps every fact: names, dates, prices, links.
  • My choice: Back to yours (or ⌘Z) puts your words back. One button, not a menu of tones.
  • My choice: a reply goes from the account the mail came to. The chip beside the buttons changes it.

4Newsletters, one click each

Newsletters have a page of their own, the ones you read least at the top. You've just unsubscribed from Spotify.

  • My choice: a newsletter is any sender whose mail carries an unsubscribe header. They skip the Inbox and land here. This is the biggest call on the page: say if you'd rather keep them in the Inbox.
  • My choice: Unsubscribe asks nothing. The request waits 10 seconds before it goes, so Undo really undoes it, and it archives what the sender sent. A sender without one-click unsubscribe opens its page in the browser.
  • My choice: the view leads with the one thing worth doing: the ones you never open.

5Your week, and what your mail adds to it

Kristina's mail asked about dinner on Saturday at 18:00. It offered the event at its top, and one click added it. Then you ask for two evenings for Layer.

  • My choice: Calendar is Radicale's, over CalDAV: both calendars in one week, each in its account's colour.
  • My choice: a mail that names a time offers the event above its text. The event remembers its mail and opens it.
  • My choice: asked to find time, Herald draws what it proposes into the week first, striped. It's made when you pick.
  • Mind: Herald doesn't see your DIB calendar, which stays in Outlook. So it plans evenings and weekends unless you tell it otherwise.
