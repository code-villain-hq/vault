Charts, tables and a grid

Almanac draws what it keeps as text, chips, bars and cards, every block the window's width. Three kinds are missing: a table to compare things across the same facts, a line for a number that changes, and a whole split into its parts. And a block should take the width it needs, so a small chart sits beside a list instead of under it.

1Preise finden: Table, light like a sheet

  • A row per shoe, a column per fact: Preis, Normal, Wo, Wasserdicht, Vegan, Größe. They're the block's facets, as a card's lines are, so Cards and Table keep the same points; ⋯ → Show as table flips it, and back.
  • Numbers stand right, in even figures, and a header sorts by its column: here by Preis, cheapest first. A click again turns it round.
  • A cell edits in place: click or Enter to change it, Enter or leaving saves, Esc goes back, Tab moves to the next cell, the arrows move between cells. New row at the foot asks a name, then the cells.
  • What needs a look is in warn: the ECCO isn't vegan, Will's and the Saola are over the 1.150 kr you set. An empty cell is a dash: nobody's said yet.
  • The shoe's name opens its page, with the sentences the cells came from. No totals row: nothing here adds up.

2Katzen: Trend, a number over time

  • Lisa's weight, as a line: the latest in large, how far it moved since the first, and a point each time she's weighed. Her Ziel is a dashed line, so the eye goes to how far off she is.
  • The pointer shows a weighing: its day and weight. A point opens what was said then.
  • Her reminder moves under it, from the profile. When Lisa wiegen comes round and you say what she weighed, the talk keeps it as the next point.
  • One line per thing: a second cat gets her own small chart beside it, on the same scale, not a second line in one.
  • It takes a third of the width, beside Gesundheit, which keeps what a good weight is.

3Karriere: Parts, and the page as a grid

  • Parts, a whole and its parts: how the 28 ads split by where you'd work. The total in the middle, a legend with each part's count and share, the largest first, in steps of the ink: no colours to learn.
  • Five parts at most; the rest folds into Andere. Parts that don't add up to a whole (an ad asks for many skills) stay Bars.
  • The page as a grid: the questions and what the market wants share a row, half each; the ads' split takes a third beside the gaps to close.
  • Rows keep the order of the blocks, left to right, top to bottom: no columns of uneven height that hide what comes next.

4The grid: twelve columns

  • Twelve columns, as most design systems have them, because twelve halves, thirds and quarters. A block takes ¼, ⅓, ½, ⅔, ¾ or the whole width; ⅔ and ¾ are there to sit beside a ⅓ or a ¼.
  • Each kind has a width it starts at: ⅓ for Parts, Trend and Chips; ½ for Bars, Questions, List and Timeline; whole for Cards, Text, Shelf, Table, Week and Today. A layout can give it another; so can you, in the block's ⋯ menu.
  • A row never ends in a hole: its blocks grow to fill it, so a ⅓ alone on its row takes the whole.
  • Each kind has a narrowest it reads at (about 18rem for a chart, 28rem for bars, questions and lists). A narrower window puts a block that would get narrower on its own row: at 1100 px Karriere is one column again, a phone always.

5What changes for Claude

  • Three new kinds, each said to the layout as what it's for: Table, things to compare across the same facts; Trend, a number kept each time it's measured; Parts, a whole split into parts that add up.
  • Table uses what Cards has: a row is a thing, a column a facet, a cell the point with that facet. A number cell has its amount.
  • Trend and Parts use what Bars has: a point's amount, and for Trend the day it happened.
  • A block has a width, which a layout sets and the ⋯ menu changes; empty is the kind's own.
  • My order to build: the grid and widths (4), Table (1), then Trend and Parts (2, 3), each in Settings → Blocks.
