How a topic answers

Each Grok bot had a description, its system prompt: who it is, in which language, how long, what it brings in, what it leaves alone. Grok Bot wrote them when it made a bot and added to them when you said "from now on". Almanac's topics get the same: what a topic sticks to becomes its brief, Almanac writes a full one, and the page stops showing it.

1The page starts with its name

  • The brief isn't on the page. It's for the answers, not for reading every time. The page starts with its name, then its blocks.
  • My choice: nothing in its place, no "Instructions" link or first line. It lives in Edit topic, where it's changed.

2Almanac writes the brief

  • Setting up a topic, Almanac writes a brief like Grok's: what it's about in a sentence or two, then how it answers: language, tone, length, what it brings in, what it checks, what it leaves alone.
  • What you wrote stays. A few words of yours ("Zimmerpflanzen, ich schicke Fotos") are kept, all of them, and Almanac fills in the rest around them.
  • Up to 4,000 characters, from 1,000. The longest Grok description is 1,618, so Tofu & Mehrwert and Karriere get theirs back in full, without the two rules that were only about Grok.
  • Edit topic shows it in a taller field, under the same name, "What it sticks to", with one line saying what it's for.

3It gets better as you talk

  • When you say how it should answer from now on, the answer folds that into the brief, and says so under it with Undo: the same box the workspace's own topic shows when it changes a topic.
  • My choice: also when you correct the same thing a second time ("wieder zu lang"), not after the first one, so a passing mood doesn't become a rule.
  • A topic's talks change only its own brief. Its name, group and icon stay with Almanac's own topic.
