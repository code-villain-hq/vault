A timeline per section

"Mach eine Zeitleiste daraus" gave Geschichte one timeline of 35 events from 1500 to 2024. Eight sections share it, so Intifada's five lines sit between Kuba's and 9/11's, and you read across topics to follow one. Here a timeline belongs to its section: a section's page starts with its own events, and the topic page only shows when each section happened.

1The page shows its sections, not every event

  • On the topic page, the timeline is a line per section: when its events begin and end, and how many there are, oldest section first. Eight lines instead of 35.
  • A section's name opens it, where its events are, as in a section opens. A section with one event shows its year and a dot; one with several, the span's longer mark.
  • Nothing moves: an event is already a point under its section's name, so the timeline Geschichte has today regroups by itself. An event with no section stays a line of its own.
  • My choice: the line opens the section, it doesn't unfold in place. There's one way into a section, as with "3 more", and the page stays short.

2A section starts with its own timeline

  • Intifada's page opens on its five events, then what it says. They're all Intifada's, so the line names no section.
  • It reads as a topic of its own: from 1967 to Israel's withdrawal from Gaza, with nothing from Kuba or 9/11 in between.

3Asking from a section adds to its timeline

  • What a talk from Intifada's page keeps goes into Intifada: when it happened at a time, a line on its timeline too. It carries the unread dot, as any new point does.
  • "Mach eine Zeitleiste daraus" there makes Intifada's alone. On the topic page it makes every section's, as it does today, and the page shows their lines.
