The page fills the window

Preise finden's cards sit in a column 56rem wide, in the middle of a wide window, with as much empty on each side. Here the page runs from the sidebar to the window's edge: the cards take as many columns as fit, and what you read keeps its line length. The sidebar's edge can be dragged, so long topic names fit.

1The page runs the width of the window

  • The page starts at the panel's left, 40px in, and ends 40px from its right, with the title and every block lined up on that left edge. Nothing is centred.
  • Cards and shelf tiles take as many columns as fit, each at least 18rem: four in this window, three or two with the talk open beside it, one on a phone.
  • The ask bar spans the page, as wide as the blocks above it.

2What you read keeps its line length

  • Writing stays at the measure, 66 characters a line, at the page's left edge: a passage across 1300px is hard to follow back to the next line.
  • Lists and timelines stay at 86 characters, so a source or a count isn't a window's width from its line. Chips wrap across the page.
  • My choice: the measure over everything full width. The room goes to what's laid side by side, the cards, not to longer lines.

3The sidebar's edge can be dragged

  • Drag the edge between the sidebar and the page, from 180px to 360px; under the pointer it shows as a line down the seam. Double-click it to go back to 224px.
  • The width stays on this Mac, for every workspace, and isn't synced: another screen may want another width. With the edge focused, ← and → move it too.
  • It doesn't fold away: narrower than 180px, it stops. Hiding the sidebar is a feature of its own.
  • My choice: it goes into the frame every app shares (studio/design/layouts/app-window.md, built once in code/kit/web). Almanac takes it now; Forge, Glyph and Raven when they next change their frame.
