A topic's About

Haar-Routine's page shows You: the facts it keeps about you so it answers for your hair. You don't read them every day, and what the topic sticks to, its brief, isn't anywhere you can see. About gathers what makes a topic answer as it does: what it sticks to, what it knows about you, and the blocks of its page. A section's page and About each get a button back to the topic.

1You leaves the page; the topic's name opens About

  • The page keeps what you use day to day: Today, Week, Your shelf and the lists. What the topic knows about you moves to About.
  • The name at the top of the page opens About, with the chevron a section's name has. In the top bar, ⋯ has About Haar-Routine in place of Edit topic…, and still has Delete topic….
  • Routine starts without a You block from now on. Haar-Routine's You moves to About once, with its points and where they came from.
  • Named About, not Overview: the page is already the overview of what's kept.

2About

  • The name, icon and group change where they are: click the name to rename the topic, the icon to pick another, the group to move it. Each change is kept as you make it, with nothing to save. The Edit topic dialog goes.
  • What it sticks to: the whole brief, which no page shows today. Click it to change it; ⌘↩ or clicking away keeps the change, and Esc drops it. Under it: when you wrote it, and when a talk last changed it.
  • What it knows about you: a line per fact, with the talk it came from. The talk's name opens it beside the page. × takes a fact out. A talk keeps new facts here, and replaces one that changes ("twice a week now").
  • On the page: its blocks in order, with how much each keeps. ⋯ moves, renames or removes one, as on the page; a block's name opens the page at it.
  • Every topic has About. One that knows nothing about you yet says so in a line. The workspace's own topic, Almanac, has only its name and what it sticks to.
  • My choice: the facts stay points, each with its source, rather than going into the brief. A point can be replaced when it changes and taken out on its own; the brief is words Claude writes whole.

3A button back to the topic

  • A section's page and About start with ← and the topic's name, above the title, where the eye starts reading. The breadcrumb and Esc still go back too.
  • A small button, as every button is: a tint, the arrow and where it goes. Settings starts the same way, with "Back to Almanac".
  • My choice: in the page, not the top bar. A back arrow beside the breadcrumb is the Mac's usual spot, but it's far from the title and says only "back", not where to.
